# Grid worlds / World lưới (Docs/SandboxPlan.md G1 to G7)

EN: A grid world is built from cell data instead of a scene. **The cells are the single truth of the ground**: what is solid, how high the floor is and which ground type lies where. Walkable area, clearance, meshes and colliders are
derived from them, never stored twice. Built in steps G1 (data, provider, chunk objects), G2 (grid navigation) G3 (edit gateway, placeable things), G4 (enemy despawn) and G5 (shared targeting). Not built yet: loot and clear counting by the world (G6), saving a running world (G7),
the chunk channel for coop is built (G8, below), the map tool and the spawner (G9), moving the roguelite rooms and removing Recast (G10). Decisions, measurements and the reasons: `Docs/Sandbox.md`.

VI: World lưới dựng từ dữ liệu ô thay cho scene. **Ô là sự thật duy nhất của nền**; vùng đi được, khoảng thoáng, mesh và collider đều dẫn xuất.

## Rules

- A world is `Width x Depth` cells (32 to 2048 per side, whole chunks of 32) of `CellSize` metres (0.25 to 1, dividing the 2 m ground cell). The edge of the world counts as solid.
- Change cells only through `PNGridWorldData`; every change bumps the version of the chunks it touches (and the neighbours across a border). Users (the mesh, later a save and the network) keep the version they last used per chunk and compare, so nobody has to clear a flag.
- The host rebuilds a chunk whose version changed (2 per frame). It removes the old colliders at once, so a ray in the same frame never sees a wall that was taken away. Chunk objects are made BEFORE the root is registered with the world presentation (registration scans every child once; measured 74 ms for 4096 children, 1.4 ms per later change); a rebuild only swaps meshes, it never adds or removes children of the registered root.
- The provider moves the root into the world's own physics scene, then `AddSlot("main", ...)`. Colliders live in that scene: the default physics scene does not see them.
- Heights are stored and meshed but the first grid navigation supports a flat floor only (`LoadGrid` refuses otherwise).

## Navigation (step G2)

A provider calls `context.SetNavigation(source)`; `PNWorldNavigation.Prepare` then builds ONE `GridGraph` in the lease from the source (never from a physics scan: the grid's own collision scan reads the default physics scene). A world declares its navigation in the
build context OR in a scene (`PNWorldNavigationSource`), never both; the Recast path is unchanged. The lease keeps its API (`TryProject`, `Start`, `StartProbe`); for a grid lease `Start` refines the funnel path before the enemy follows it, with
margin = body radius + the follower's corner tolerance + 0.02 (`PNEnemyMove.CornerToleranceValue`), including a repath that has only two points. First release limits: flat floor, the scan runs on the calling frame (a 2048 x 2048 world takes seconds), one profile (`ground-small`: radius 0.2, height 1.4) per world.

## Editing and placeable things (step G3)

After the world is built, cells change ONLY through `PNGridEditor` (`Core/GridWorld/PNGridEdits.cs`, pure): a `PNGridEditBatch` of solid / ground / height edits is validated whole (inside the world, a palette ground, at most 4096 cells, a 129-cell extent), queued (65536 cells at most) and applied on the authority, at most `PNGridWorldController.CellsPerFrame` (512) cells a frame, in the order queued. The cells that changed come back as CLUSTERS (`PNGridClusters`: cells in touching 8x8 tiles, each cluster the box of its own cells; one box around two far edits measured 411 ms against 15 ms), the clearance field is recomputed for each cluster only, and the chunk versions change so the host rebuilds the chunks. Height edits are refused while the navigation is flat only (`AllowHeight`).

`PNGridWorldController` (`Game/GridWorld/`, one per world on the root of the grid host; `PNGridWorldController.Find(world)`) owns the editor and `PNGridPlacements`, pumps the editor every frame on the authority and hands the changed regions to the navigation lease, at most `NavRegionsPerFrame` (8) a frame. The lease (`PNWorldNavigationLease.ApplyGridChanges`) updates walkability of each region grown by the source's `Influence`, then `RecalculateConnectionsInRegion`, in an A* work item; it numbers the changes (`ChangeSerial`) and `PNEnemyMove` repaths when a change lies near its route or within 16 m of the body (`ChangedNear`), so a wall put in front of a walking enemy makes it go around and a wall taken away gives it the short way again.

Placeable things: kind `placeable` of the `instances` registry (`PNPlaceableDefinition`, payload: `footprint {width,depth}` 1 to 8, `blocksPath`, `blocksSight`, `breakable`, `health`, `spawnBlock`, `spawnRateScale`, `safetyRadius`, `params`; unknown fields refused). A definition names a LOGIC class (`IPNPlaceableLogic.Broken`): `project_n:placeable_vanish` (nothing happens) and `project_n:placeable_leave_ground` (params `ground`: repaints the footprint). Shipped samples: `placeable_vanish` (1x1, health 30), `placeable_leave_ground` (2x2, sand, health 20), `placeable_marker` (1x1, does not block the way, `spawnBlock`, safety radius 6 m; drawn as a small yellow marker with no collider). A thing that blocks the way turns its footprint cells solid THROUGH THE GATEWAY, so the wall mesh, the colliders, the clearance field and the graph all follow from the cells and nothing blocks a body that the grid does not know about. `PNGridPlacements` keeps the list (`TryPlace`, `Damage`, `Break`, `Remove`, `At`, `InRegion`); a direction 0 to 3 turns the footprint a quarter each (an odd one swaps its sides).

Sync commands (`Game/GridWorld/PNGridCommands.cs`), sent to the world object `grid` (the root of the host): `project_n:grid_place` `{def,x,z,dir}` and `project_n:grid_break` `{x,z}` (one hit of `HitDamage` 5). The authority checks a living player of the sender in that world within 6.5 m of the footprint, a registered placeable and free cells; anything else is dropped without a word. The rules are in `PNGridCommands.ApplyPlace` / `ApplyBreak`, callable with a position. Clients see the result only once the chunk channel exists (step G8). Not built: free (off-grid) placement, `blocksSight` effects, health or damage from tools and enemies, saving placed things (G7).

Measured (Play Mode in the Editor, 2048 x 2048 world with a graph and no chunk objects): the world with its graph is ready in about 5 s and costs about 0.5 GB managed memory (Unity reports about 2.2 GB allocated in the Editor); one placement costs 0.04 ms on the cell side and 1.1 to 2.3 ms on the graph side (a constant cost of the A* update, not of the world's size; the plan asked for 1 ms and the cleanest run measured 1.16 to 1.31 ms); 256 things spread over the world are 256 regions and 65 to 100 ms of graph work, so they are spread over 32 frames of at most 3 ms; 256 things in one block are one region and 2.4 ms. Checks: `Tests/EveEngine/PNGridEditTests.cs` (79 checks) and `Assets/Tests/Core/PNGridEditPlayReview.cs` (`Begin(dir)`: 65 checks with a real enemy; `Begin(dir, large: true)`: the 2048 x 2048 timings).

## Enemy life cycle and targeting (steps G4, G5)

The world feature `project_n:despawn` (added to the two sandbox test worlds) makes the provider create `PNDespawnService` (`Assets/Game/Entity/Lifetime/`); the roguelite rooms do not have it. Every 0.5 s, on the authority, it measures each enemy's flat distance to the nearest living player: inside `near` (25 m) the timer resets, beyond `far` (35 m) it runs, between the two it holds, and at `seconds` (10) the enemy is retired through the entity host. Exempt (role data), held (`PNEntityLifetime.Hold`), executing-action and `countsForClear` enemies are skipped; no living player pauses the timer. An enemy with a `leash` walks home (`PNEnemyHome`) when the timer starts or when it strays with no player near.
`PNWorldEntityIndex` (`Assets/Core/Entity/Shared/Lifetime/PNEntityIndex.cs`) is the chunk-bucketed candidate list of a world, shared by targeting and despawn; it returns candidates only, the consumer still checks ready and alive. `PNEnemyTargeting` (`Assets/Game/Entity/Enemy/`) is the one place the Star enemies find, keep and rank targets; in a grid world its line of sight is `PNGridSight.Clear` (a walk over the cells, solid cells block), elsewhere the `Wall` ray. Checks: `Tests/EveEngine/PNLifetimeTargetingTests.cs` and `Assets/Tests/Core/PNLifetimeTargetingPlayReview.cs` (`Begin(dir)`: 65 checks).

## Saving and restoring a running world (step G7)

A world with the feature `project_n:world_save` (the two sandbox test worlds) gets `PNGridWorldSaveService` (`Assets/Game/GridWorld/`), on the authority only. It is idle until a mode calls `Configure(store, key)` with a `PNWorldFileStore` folder and a key, so nothing touches the real save folder by itself and a test points it at a temporary folder.

- **What is saved**: the ground types (coarse cells), floor heights and solid cells of every chunk, the placed things (id, definition, cell, direction, health) and where each living player stands (key `owner:<OwnerId>`). Players' bodies and ordinary enemies are not saved; NPCs and named entities are not saved yet (nothing in the game needs them, they need their own serializer).
- **Format** (`Assets/Core/GridWorld/PNGridWorldSave.cs`): the portable `PNWorldSaveEnvelope` (one state record, `world` entry of kind `project_n:grid_world`), the descriptor in the envelope, one blob per chunk (format byte, deflate, Adler-32 trailer; about 3 KB raw, tens of bytes when flat). Parsing is strict; a save of other ground types, another size, or another rules/content version is refused whole (`PNWorldSaveEnvelope` compatibility).
- **Capture**: on the main thread, only the chunks whose `ChunkVersion` moved, at most `BudgetMilliseconds` (2 ms) per frame; afterwards the versions are checked again and a chunk that changed during the copy is copied again (three rounds). A worker thread encodes the copies, builds the snapshot and writes it (atomic revisions, two kept). Autosave every `Interval` (300 s) when something changed, `SaveNow()`, and a blocking `Flush()` when the world is unloaded.
- **Restore**: `PNGridWorldSaves.ReadAsync` reads and validates the file on a worker; `PNGridWorldSaves.Restore` creates the world STAGED from the save's descriptor plus a one-time `restore` token (the decoded save waits in `PNGridRestoreStaging`); the provider applies 32 chunks per frame, restores the placed things with their ids and health, and the service starts with the save as its baseline (not dirty). The caller publishes the staged world when it is ready. The players' saved places are in `service.Players`; putting the players there is the mode's job.
- Checks: `Tests/EveEngine/PNGridWorldSaveTests.cs` (smoke), `Assets/Tests/Core/PNGridSavePlayReview.cs` (`Begin(dir)` the maze world, `Begin(dir, large: true)` a 1024 x 1024 world).

## Chunk channel for coop (G8, G8b; NetworkVersion 18)
- **G8b (bulk edits)**: a chunk the client holds is brought up to date with a DELTA (the cells that changed, current values, plus the Adler-32 of the chunk afterwards) or the WHOLE chunk, whichever is smaller; a delta of at most 256 bytes is sent without comparing. The cells come from a per-chunk journal (256 cells; beyond that the whole chunk goes) keyed on a content revision (`ChunkRevisionAt`, bumped only by that chunk's own cells). A client applies a delta only on the exact base revision and checks the checksum; a mismatch sends `PNGridChunkResync` and the host sends the chunk whole (`PNGridChunkSender.MarkLost`). Whole blobs come from a per-world cache (`PNGridChunkBlobCache`, newest blob per chunk, 4 MiB, LRU). `PNGridEditor.SubmitGroup` (all-or-nothing, `PNGridEditBatch.SolidRegionBatches` cuts a region into 64x64 batches) and `BeginGroup`/`EndGroup` hold updates to clients up to 0.5 s so a blast arrives in one piece. The controller applies edits on a time budget (`EditBudgetMilliseconds` 1.5 ms, at least `CellsPerFrame` 512 cells, at most 4096). A grid world has one player entry (`PNGridWorldProvider.AddPlayerSpawn`: `PNPlayerSpawnPoint` `player` in slot `main`, character `project_n:eve_2`, free ground near the middle; a transfer or mode puts players in through it; a map tool replaces it in G9). `ApplyPlace` refuses a blocking placeable over a body ("Something stands there."); a body that ends up inside newly solid cells is moved to the nearest free spot (`PNGridBodies`, `PNMove.RelocateToPoint`, which also works on a stunned or held body and cancels the running command). The host moves bodies it owns; a body a CLIENT owns is moved by that client when the walls reach its copy of the world (`PNGridChunkReceiver.PushOwnedOut`, after each applied chunk and every 0.25 s), and the host follows from its pose reports. Checks: `Tests/EveEngine/PNGridDeltaTests.cs`, `Assets/Tests/Coop/PNGridChunkPlayChecks.cs`, `PNGridEditPlayReview.Begin(dir, false, bodies: true)`.

- **Client world**: a client never generates the ground. `PNWorldOffer.Descriptor` (JSON, no restore token) brings the shape; the provider on a replica (`!Session.HasAuthority`) builds an EMPTY world of that shape, makes the chunk objects, and waits until the host has sent the first set (the chunks within 3 of the client's players) and they are applied and built. Only then is the world Ready (`PNWorldLoaded`). No navigation graph, no save/despawn services on a replica. Chunks not yet received look like plain ground type 0.
- **Host** (`Assets/Coop/PNGridChunkChannel.cs`, pure part `Core/GridWorld/PNGridChunkChannel.cs`): per client and world a `PNGridChunkSender` remembers the version of each chunk the client has. Each frame it picks the nearest chunks (Chebyshev, around the client's own actors; else the world's other actors; else the middle) inside a ring of 8 chunks, plus every chunk the client already has that changed since (the WHOLE chunk again, not cell edits: a blob of tens of bytes to ~3.4 KB, nothing to order against). Up to 16 chunks / 16 KB per packet, 4 packets and 32 KB per client per frame, 1.5 ms of encoding, and the adapter's shared `TakeSendBudget`. A packet the transport refuses stays held and goes first next frame. The packet that completes the first set carries `Final`.
- **Client**: `PNGridChunkInbox` (8 MiB bound, a newer offer resets it, a packet of an old offer is dropped) filled by the network layer; `PNGridChunkReceiver` decodes 8 chunks / 2 ms per frame into the cells and the chunk host rebuilds the changed chunks. A damaged blob fails the world. A phone refuses a world over 512 x 512 cells (`LastFailure`, not routed through the localization catalog; not run on a phone).
- Not sent: placed-thing markers of things that do not block the way (the controller draws them on the host only); the clearance field (navigation is host-only).
- Checks: `Tests/EveEngine/PNGridChunkChannelTests.cs` (smoke, 78 checks incl. a 1024 x 1024 stream) and the three-process Player run `Assets/Tests/Coop/PNGridChunkPlayChecks.cs` (host, client, late client, rejoin; runner `.utmp/GridChunk/RunGridChunk.ps1`; the Player is built from the main project to `G:\ProjectN_Camera\GridChunkPlayer`).
